## ELI5
Under the board there is a drawer labelled "Bookkeeping 133 · Parked 20 · Deferred 0". Open it and all 153 cards pour out as one giant column glued to the page. The drawer has no height limit and no scroll region of its own, so your finger scrolls the whole page, never the list — the last card is a long way down and the board disappears above you. The pop-up ticket drawer already does this right: it has a ceiling and scrolls inside itself. This fix gives the shelf the same kind of ceiling (about half to two-thirds of the screen, measured in the unit that tracks the phone's real visible area), makes the list scroll inside that box with a thin visible scrollbar so you can tell it scrolls, keeps the "BOOKKEEPING / PARKED" label pinned while you scroll through its group, and proves it with a test that actually swipes on a phone-sized screen and was shown to fail on today's build.

## Root cause (verified against `752a531` source)
- `app/globals.css:590-596` `.ak-shelf[open] > .ak-shelf__body` sets `display:flex; flex-direction:column; gap; padding; border-top` and NOTHING that bounds height or engages overflow. With `overflow` at its initial value the element is not a scroll container: `scrollTop` cannot change, `scrollHeight == clientHeight`, and any gesture inside it is taken by the page. Confirmed — matches the brief.
- The good pattern exists at `app/globals.css:1467-1474` `.ak-drawer__body` (`overflow-y:auto; overscroll-behavior:contain; -webkit-overflow-scrolling:touch`) inside an `86dvh` column. Its `flex:1 1 auto; min-height:0` exist only because that body is a flex CHILD of a bounded column; the shelf body will be its own bounded container, so those two do not transfer.
- Containment invariant: `html` (L105) and `body` (L117) both pin `overflow-x:hidden`. Setting `overflow-y` to a non-visible value makes the computed `overflow-x` non-visible too (`auto`), so the shelf body needs its own explicit horizontal pin or a wide child would surface a horizontal scroll inside the panel.
- Why the guards were blind: the S0 fixture store yields ~14 shelf tickets (9 bookkeeping, 2 deferred, plus status-derived parked) — a 14-card body is shorter than any sane ceiling, so no existing spec could ever observe the wall. Production is 133 + 20. The 2026-09-05 lesson exactly.
- `Shelf.tsx` needs no logic change: it already renders `.ak-shelf__body > .ak-shelf__group > .ak-shelf__group-label + .ak-shelf__cards`, and the closed-state `display:none` rule (L586-588, the AC-1.5 `scrollHeight == 0` contract) must stay.

## Intent (what & why — never how)
**What**
1. The opened shelf body is its OWN bounded, self-scrolling panel: its on-screen height is a fraction of the visual viewport (a `dvh`-class unit per the POV — never a fixed pixel cap, never `vh`), short enough that the board strip above and the page's bottom edge stay visible as spatial anchors, tall enough to show several cards at once so scrolling reads as paging a list.
2. Every shelf ticket is reachable by scrolling INSIDE that panel, by real touch on a phone and by wheel on desktop, and scrolling inside the panel does not move the page.
3. The panel cannot introduce horizontal overflow — inside itself or at the document root — even if a future card is wider than the panel.
4. The panel's scrollability is discoverable at a glance: a deliberately visible thin scrollbar (the one justified break from the board's hidden-scrollbar convention — a vertical wall of full-width cards has no "peek" cue) and, if achievable without hiding that rail or adding scroll listeners, the POV's dark-ground edge fade.
5. The per-kind group label stays pinned at the panel's top edge while its group scrolls under it (133 bookkeeping cards would otherwise lose their heading on the first flick), with an opaque background so cards do not ghost through it.
6. The closed state, the tap-to-open behaviour, the remembered open/closed preference, and the column board are unchanged.

**Why**: the operator cannot reach most of the shelf on a phone; the board's own doctrine (the drawer pattern at L1467, the root containment invariant) already says how such a panel behaves; and the S1 round shipped this exact wall because its fixture could not produce it (the cairn stone above).

## Design commitments (from the banked POV — leg 1)
- Height: viewport-relative in a visual-viewport unit, roughly half to two-thirds; the inline shelf claims LESS than the takeover drawer's 86.
- Scroll wiring mirrors `.ak-drawer__body`'s mechanics (contain + touch momentum), NOT its flex wiring.
- Explicit horizontal pin on the body alongside vertical scrolling.
- Visible thin scrollbar tinted from the console's faint/line tokens on a transparent track; edge fade is secondary and must never mask the rail.
- Sticky, opaque (`--panel`), hairline-bordered group labels.
- Rejected: virtualising the shelf (not needed at 153; file if INP ever flags it); paginating the shelf (hides the count the summary already shows); a fixed-px cap (breaks on the fold/phone tiers); copying `min-height:0`/`flex:1` from the drawer (wrong container role).
